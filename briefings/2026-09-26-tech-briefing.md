# JARVIS Daily Tech Briefing — Saturday, September 26, 2026

**Lead — [Constrained Decoding Eliminates Structural Failures in Small LLMs but Reveals a Scale-Dependent Semantic Gap (arXiv:2609.23742)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23742) is the measured version of the exact claim the extractor's design leans on, and it draws the line precisely where your own bake-off drew it.** Benchmarking five models across three families (0.6B–4B, squarely the extractor's weight class) on 14 structured-output tasks under three decoding regimes — native, Outlines, XGrammar — they find constrained decoding drives **schema validity from 78.6–92.9% straight to 100%** across every model, and type-conversion errors become fully recoverable. But **semantic correctness does not move the same way**: instruction-following failures (multi-step function calling, getting the right value into a validly-shaped slot) resist constrained decoding entirely and scale with model capacity — a "**scale-dependent semantic gap**" that grammar compilation cannot close. That is the field confirming, with a number, what the field addendum already found by hand: llama.cpp's contract-2 grammar took nine of eleven runs to validity 1.0000, and the remaining work was F1 on `relation_id`/`polarity`, not validity — structure and correctness are different axes, and only one of them is a decoding problem. **Denominator: extractor squarely (0.6B–4B, llama.cpp-class constrained decoding) — the box has no constrained-decoding path at all.**
