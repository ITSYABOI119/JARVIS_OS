## Voice — verification, diarization, ASR

- **[Fast and Robust On-Device Speaker Diarization: Relative Minimum Cluster Size for Stride-Accelerated Pipelines (arXiv:2606.08505)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08505) — never briefed, and it names a failure mode your clustering rules could share by construction.** Yamaguchi accelerates a Pyannote 3.1 pipeline on consumer hardware (RTX 5070 Ti; Apple M4) by striding the embedding step, and finds the accuracy loss is **not** in the embedder — it is **speaker under-counting during clustering, caused by a *fixed* minimum cluster size interacting with the reduced number of embeddings per speaker.** The fix is to make it relative: `mcs = round(f · n)`, `f ≈ 0.01`, adapting to the embedding budget of the recording. One value of `f` recovers VoxConverse DER to **0.079 (~89 % of the lost accuracy)** while leaving AMI flat, at up to **12.2× speedup** over their CAM++ baseline. **The transfer is the shape, not the constant: your people layer uses fixed counts too — a cluster earns personhood after *3 distinct days*, the owner merge needs *≥ 10 s* of accumulated embedded speech — against a corpus the M1d take measured at 2.0 % speech, with 43.5 % of that in runs under 2 s.** A fixed threshold on a variable embedding budget is the same hazard, and it fails the same way: too few clusters, the quiet household member absorbed into a louder one. Denominator: extractor-class hardware (a GPU or a laptop), nothing for the box. [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.08505)
- **[SpeechGym (arXiv:2608.26432, Amazon/UIUC) — an audio-native gym for training voice agents, and its two named failure modes are both yours.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26432)** Two omni-modal models converse in native audio with no ASR/TTS boundary, over the unmodified tasks and success checks of a text agentic benchmark, so modality is the only variable. **Finding 1: the failures speech introduces are perceptual, not reasoning — "the agent picks the right tool and the right argument slot but fills it with a value misheard from the waveform," which cascades into a failed call, a retry, and a wasted step budget.** **Finding 2, the one to keep: "under an insistent caller the agent performs an unauthorised write and ends the episode believing it helped."** Goal 8's rule is commands obeyed from the owner's verified voice only, no wake word — i.e. an always-listening agent that will eventually act. This is a measured account of an agent acting on a misheard argument and of an agent being talked into an action it was not authorised to take, with the environment labelling both for free. Method note worth stealing: outcome-only GRPO was gradient-starved (nearly every rollout group failed identically); a **per-turn process reward crediting each successful tool call** restored variance. Denominator: extractor-class (GPU, omni-modal models). *Never briefed; from the stale `/new` batch dated 28 Aug.*

## Memory & Extraction

- **[Who Are They to Each Other? Multi-Agent Reasoning for Speaker Relationship Inference (arXiv:2609.09628, Sep 9, JHU/CLSP — Villalba, Thebaud, Dehak)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09628) — the people layer, as a published task, with an external scoreboard. Missed by three sweeps of the same listing.** It is *training-free*: LLM agents propose, challenge and adjudicate relationship judgments, in two designs — **Multi-Role Multi-Agent Debate** (agents given complementary roles or social-theory-grounded perspectives rather than one undifferentiated viewpoint) and **Multi-Agent Compete** (pairwise adjudication, eliminate the weaker, keep the most defensible). Evaluated on Seamless Interaction across modality settings, for both binary classification and fine-grained relationship-detail prediction. **Three things to take, and the last is the one that calibrates goal 8.** (1) The task your design calls "JARVIS must GUESS, over days, that the second voice is his wife" is a named, under-explored research task with a benchmark — your synthetic pre-registered band (the spouse edge surfaced in ≥ 8 of 10 households, 0 wrong relations) is not the only possible yardstick. (2) Their structure is *select-and-adjudicate*, which is the same instinct as your static predicate registry — the LLM argues, something else decides. (3) **The honest ceiling, from their own results: the task is "challenging even for people"; LLMs sometimes beat human annotators in text-included settings but are *less competitive in the audio setting*, and they conclude "acoustic cues are not yet fully captured by current models."** Your relationship inference runs over transcripts plus voice clusters — i.e. mostly the text-included regime, which is the half that works; the acoustic half, where the field is weak, is where the wife-guess actually lives. No numbers quoted: the abstract reports directions, not digits, and I did not read the body. Denominator: extractor-class (multi-agent LLM inference), nothing for the box.
- **[AudioSpan (arXiv:2608.26431) — a long-form audio benchmark whose headline finding is the household-transcript problem stated as a result.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26431)** 10 minutes to 2+ hours of audio, 3,240 questions across perception / understanding / reasoning, two question paths (native QA extracted from content; anchor QA with acoustic anchors planted into the audio and the chain scored only to the first error). Across 12 large audio-language models: **"the hard part comes before reasoning: distilling a few relevant facts from a long, redundant signal. This difficulty grows with audio length and falls hardest on perception, especially temporal grounding."** That is the M1d six-hour take in one sentence — 437.5 s of speech in 21,600 s, 20 of 24 chunks holding none — and it is an argument for your architecture rather than against it: you do not ask a model to comprehend six hours, you extract typed facts from turns and let the store do retrieval. The number to carry is that the models which *do* try the end-to-end version fail at exactly the step your pipeline replaces with code. *Never briefed; also from the stale 28 Aug `/new` batch.* [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/holvan/AudioSpan)
