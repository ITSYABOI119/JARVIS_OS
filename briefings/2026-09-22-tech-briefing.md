## Voice — verification, diarization, ASR

- **pyannoteAI shipped Precision-3 (17 Sep), and the gain is concentrated in exactly the thing your people layer does.** Average DER across 15 benchmarks **16.02 → 14.35 (−10.4%)**, and the changelog attributes **53% of that gain to speaker attribution specifically — wrong-speaker assignment down 18.7%.** New per-segment outputs `speakerProbability` / `speechProbability` / `crosstalkProbability` replace the frame-level confidence, and `vadSensitivity` + `crosstalkSensitivity` split VAD from overlap control. Default switches 1 Oct; Precision-2 deprecated 15 Oct; on-prem `vadSensitivity` **inverts the scale** of the old `vad_aggressiveness`. This is the commercial line, not open `pyannote.audio` — but a per-segment speaker-confidence output is precisely the signal your cluster/merge rules currently do not have. [changelog](https://www.pyannote.ai/changelog)
- **[Understanding Hyperspherical Geometry of ECAPA-TDNN Embedding (arXiv:2609.24688, IRCAM, announced 22 Sep)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24688) — this is the geometry underneath your single stored threshold, and it says the space is worse-behaved than the threshold assumes.** Analysing ECAPA classifier prototypes with rotation-invariant angular statistics, they find standard **angular-margin training induces angular concentration and a substantial reduction in effective dimensionality**; two geometric regularisers (hinged Riesz log-energy, effective-dimension maximisation) restore isotropy with "configuration-dependent" effects on recognition. **Anisotropic, concentrated prototypes are the condition under which one global τ\* = 0.3461 behaves inconsistently across speakers** — which is the same nerve as the retracted 12.0 s minimum, approached from the representation rather than from duration. No EER figures in the abstract.
- **[Entropy-aware logistic regression for fusion of speaker recognition systems (arXiv:2609.23727, Avignon, IEEE SLT 2026, announced 22 Sep)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23727) — a per-utterance reliability signal that needs no duration table.** Conventional score fusion assigns fixed per-system weights and ignores how reliable any given enrolment or test utterance is; this adds a **model-entropy uncertainty term** so weighting becomes utterance-dependent. **The transferable part works with one system and no fusion at all:** entropy as a confidence gate deciding whether a turn's score should be trusted against your threshold — the principled replacement for the duration grid M1a.4 retracted for small-n. Abstract states no numbers.
- **[Foreground Voice Activity Detection (arXiv:2609.19856, announced 18 Sep)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19856) — enrollment-free dominant-speaker VAD at 1–2 ms per-frame CPU latency.** Only the *dominant* speaker (defined by sustained presence, not instantaneous loudness) is positive; reduces to ordinary VAD when one speaker is present. Their claim is that selectivity comes from **supervision, not architecture** — an automatic augmentation recipe mixing competing speakers against foreground-only labels, no human annotation — and Mamba and LSTM backbones perform on par while longer-context attention does not help. Mamba-FVAD beats commercial VADs *and* enrollment-based speaker-aware systems on selectivity, with a new BG-FAR metric gated by foreground F1. Relevant to a six-hour take that is 2.0% speech with 43.5% of it in runs under 2 s.
- **[Voice-Light (arXiv:2609.20995, announced 21 Sep)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20995) — a full-duplex voice agent whose headline is a *withdrawn* capability claim, pre-registered.** On 1,673 real-conversation silence candidates a learned end-of-turn checkpoint held a 2.70% false-cutoff rate but reached only **12.53% recall against 95.60% for a plain Silero timing policy** — so they keep a hybrid controller and explicitly decline to claim a learned replacement. Median 758 ms from VAD endpoint to first audio over 36 turns, labelled an instrumented case study rather than an evaluation. Pairs with Saturday-the-12th's end-of-turn item (turn ends are prosodic, not semantic): **two independent results now say do not reach past Silero for turn boundaries.** Also worth the click for the invariant that cancelled audio must never enter durable history — the transcript-integrity analogue of delete-raw-audio-after-transcription.
- Two more, one line each. **[MECT (arXiv:2609.24061, 22 Sep)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24061)** adds Mixture-of-Experts to a CNN-Transformer speaker-verification backbone and, more usefully, reports a causal-retrained **streaming variant holding performance at a 100 ms chunk** — an alternative to ECAPA's wait-for-the-turn-to-end pooling. **[Word Timestamps and Speaker Attribution with a Non-Autoregressive LLM (arXiv:2609.15218, 15 Sep)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15218)** adds timestamps and speaker labels to an *already-produced* transcript, claiming SOTA timestamp accuracy and best cpWER **one to two orders of magnitude faster** than the autoregressive equivalent — i.e. attribution as a post-pass over faster-whisper output rather than a parallel pipeline.

## Memory & Extraction

- **[AIM + MUMBench (arXiv:2609.12320, 11 Sep)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12320) — privacy-scoped memory for a shared household agent, and its own numbers are the interesting half.** Classifies each memory as *private* (index-level access control, owner-only retrieval) or *public* rather than siloing per user; MUMBench is claimed as the first dataset evaluating retrieval/creation/update/deletion together in a multi-user setting. **96.0% visibility-classification accuracy but only 58.8% strict operation accuracy** — it decides who-may-see-what well and gets the actual memory operation right barely more than half the time. **Lead — [Memory That Looks Forward (arXiv:2609.22091, announced today)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22091) is a mechanism your store is missing, and its scope finding is more valuable than its headline number.** The premise: retrieval over a personal memory store is *retrospective* — it surfaces what resembles the query and is blind to what the user has committed to do. The fix is a **commitment ledger** of dated or trigger-conditioned entries; memory items linked to a firing entry get a salience boost **blended multiplicatively into embedding retrieval, so relevance stays sovereign**, and it costs **zero inference at query time**. On a synthetic set (48 blind-authored dialogues, 175 tasks) recall@5 on the hard stratum goes **0.000 → 0.955**, with **zero false boosts across 53 resolved-commitment tasks**.

**The scope finding is the part to keep:** blind authorship showed **only 17–29% of naturally phrased commitment-trigger pairs defeat embedding similarity** — so the term matters on a real minority of cases and its whole burden is *doing no harm on the other 71–83%*, which they measure rather than assume. That is your MS1a.4 shape exactly (transfer 91.67% met; the residual was crowding and ranking, not a general deficit), and it is the discipline you already apply: a lane earns its place by a measured no-harm control, not by its best-case number. Two honest caveats the authors state themselves — the eval set is author-constructed, and TriggerBench proper is committed follow-up once its data ships. **Denominator: memory-store-class (pure ranking logic, no model, runs beside the FTS5/vector lanes) — nothing for the box.** Code and data: `github.com/Groffitti/memory-that-looks-forward`.

## Memory & Extraction

- **[PII-TRACE (arXiv:2609.22200)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22200) — a 0.6B PII detector trained for multi-turn conversations, and the benchmark says every off-the-shelf detector you might have reached for is inadequate.** 13,148 synthetic multi-turn dialogues, 13 languages, character-level spans and **identifier clusters** (every mention of a recurring identifier across turns, which is the property that matters for a household transcript). Across eleven baselines *including frontier LLMs*, **no detector achieves full entity-level coverage without substantial false positives on PII-free conversations, and single-pass reading loses a third of the gold characters on long dialogues.** Their `PII-Tracer` (0.6B, conversation-level supervision) takes the top entity-level coverage. Directly relevant to the owner's manual purge of non-household transcripts: a recurring-identifier-aware detector is the tool that makes "purge by hand" tractable, and the negative result is the reason not to bolt a record-level detector onto a transcript. **Denominator: extractor-class (0.6B fits the 2070 trivially, alongside the embedder) — nothing for the box.**
- **[Schematize (arXiv:2609.22209, EMNLP 2026 demo)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22209) — an agentic system that generates and refines an *extraction schema* interactively, which is the predicate-registry problem stated from the other end.** Four stages: a clarification dialogue eliciting implicit intent, iterative schema generation, **data-grounded refinement that tests the schema against real documents**, and chat-based post-editing. Legal-tailored but explicitly domain-agnostic, pip-installable. Read it as a method, not a dependency: your registry is deliberately static and human-reviewed (the K-b select-never-synthesise boundary), and nothing here should change that — but *data-grounded refinement of a schema against a held-out corpus* is precisely the missing loop between "MS2a-1 measured `person.relation_to` F1 falling 0.4054 → 0.3467" and knowing whether the predicate or the contract is at fault. **Denominator: extractor-class (tooling, not a model).**
- **[SCoP (arXiv:2609.22213)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22213) — externalise temporal decisions into executable constraints *before* inference, rather than letting a model resolve them.** For temporal-KG QA: separate the answer-seeking pattern from the temporal anchor, conservatively ground both to canonical entities/relations, translate temporal intent into **executable constraints over normalised point and interval ranges**, then deterministically filter candidates. **0.825 Hits@1 on MultiTQ, 0.761 on TimelineCronQ-R, with no task-specific parameter updates.** This is the closest external evidence yet for the bitemporal half of `PHASE_7_MEMORY_DESIGN.md` — newest-`valid_from`-wins *by comparison, never by a model's judgement*. Their framing ("existing methods leave temporal admissibility implicit in model reasoning, allowing locally relevant but invalid facts into the answer context") is your R3 rule with a benchmark attached.
- **[Context Poisoning as Extreme-Value Attention Interference (arXiv:2609.22101)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22101) — a theoretical account of why a preamble gets worse as you add facts, with the scaling law.** Under a softmax-retrieval abstraction, holding accuracy above base rate requires the evidence margin to scale as **Ω(√log N)** where *N* is the **effective distractor count**, not raw context length; the **same-format** distractor condition produced the largest accuracy drop of any construction tested at fixed length. Two things land: your `G3_MAX_FACTS = 2` cap is better-founded than it looked (small *N* by construction), and **same-format is exactly what a two-fact preamble of identically-rendered stored answers is** — the `[23:00110]` shape, where both candidates held the identical stored answer at cosine 0.804. Argues for evidence bottlenecks and retrieve-then-reason over bigger preambles.
- **One line, adjacent:** [AdaMem (arXiv:2609.22100)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22100) allocates a fixed memory-token budget *by relevance* instead of uniformly across retrieved passages (+3.2 sub-string-match points at 16× compression, average relative gain rising to 14.6% at 64×). Not your architecture — you inject verbatim spans, not learned soft memories — but "spend the budget where the relevance score says" is the same instinct as a per-fact preamble cap that is not equal-split.

## Privacy & Law

- **[Evaluating Personal Information Output from Conversational Interactions (arXiv:2609.22204, IIAI-AAI 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22204) — a small study that names your inferred-facts risk precisely.** 15 participants, GPT-5.2 Instant vs Thinking, outputs classified Fact / Inference / Confidence. Findings: model design barely changes the tendency; **Fact output is conservative while Inference is not**; core identifying attributes are treated cautiously while **behavioural and linguistic attributes come out accurate across both**; and **Holistic Profile, Psychological and Cognitive attributes are readily inferred with *no null outputs* in the Inference type — i.e. the system never declines to guess.** Your design uses inferred facts without confirmation by the owner's explicit decision; this is external evidence for what the inference layer will volunteer about household members if nothing bounds it, and an argument that confidence-surfacing (your ≥ 0.80 threshold) is doing load-bearing work rather than decoration. n=15, exploratory, Japanese cohort — a caution, not a measurement.
- **[Privacy–Personalization Trade-offs (arXiv:2609.22112)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22112) — the quantified cost of stripping stylometric signal.** On LaMP-7 with 250 users, outputs conditioned on original profiles are near-indistinguishable from human ground truth; conditioned on **anonymised** profiles (demographic identifiers, cultural references, personal details and informal linguistic cues neutralised), preference collapses to **13.0%** while **semantic context preservation stays at 94.8%**. Relevant to the separate preference-and-style model in your design: style *is* the identifying signal, so a household style model is a re-identification surface in a way a fact store is not — and the meaning survives the scrub while the voice does not.
