## Voice — verification, diarization, ASR

- **pyannoteAI shipped Precision-3 (17 Sep), and the gain is concentrated in exactly the thing your people layer does.** Average DER across 15 benchmarks **16.02 → 14.35 (−10.4%)**, and the changelog attributes **53% of that gain to speaker attribution specifically — wrong-speaker assignment down 18.7%.** New per-segment outputs `speakerProbability` / `speechProbability` / `crosstalkProbability` replace the frame-level confidence, and `vadSensitivity` + `crosstalkSensitivity` split VAD from overlap control. Default switches 1 Oct; Precision-2 deprecated 15 Oct; on-prem `vadSensitivity` **inverts the scale** of the old `vad_aggressiveness`. This is the commercial line, not open `pyannote.audio` — but a per-segment speaker-confidence output is precisely the signal your cluster/merge rules currently do not have. [changelog](https://www.pyannote.ai/changelog)
- **[Understanding Hyperspherical Geometry of ECAPA-TDNN Embedding (arXiv:2609.24688, IRCAM, announced 22 Sep)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24688) — this is the geometry underneath your single stored threshold, and it says the space is worse-behaved than the threshold assumes.** Analysing ECAPA classifier prototypes with rotation-invariant angular statistics, they find standard **angular-margin training induces angular concentration and a substantial reduction in effective dimensionality**; two geometric regularisers (hinged Riesz log-energy, effective-dimension maximisation) restore isotropy with "configuration-dependent" effects on recognition. **Anisotropic, concentrated prototypes are the condition under which one global τ\* = 0.3461 behaves inconsistently across speakers** — which is the same nerve as the retracted 12.0 s minimum, approached from the representation rather than from duration. No EER figures in the abstract.
- **[Entropy-aware logistic regression for fusion of speaker recognition systems (arXiv:2609.23727, Avignon, IEEE SLT 2026, announced 22 Sep)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23727) — a per-utterance reliability signal that needs no duration table.** Conventional score fusion assigns fixed per-system weights and ignores how reliable any given enrolment or test utterance is; this adds a **model-entropy uncertainty term** so weighting becomes utterance-dependent. **The transferable part works with one system and no fusion at all:** entropy as a confidence gate deciding whether a turn's score should be trusted against your threshold — the principled replacement for the duration grid M1a.4 retracted for small-n. Abstract states no numbers.
- **[Foreground Voice Activity Detection (arXiv:2609.19856, announced 18 Sep)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19856) — enrollment-free dominant-speaker VAD at 1–2 ms per-frame CPU latency.** Only the *dominant* speaker (defined by sustained presence, not instantaneous loudness) is positive; reduces to ordinary VAD when one speaker is present. Their claim is that selectivity comes from **supervision, not architecture** — an automatic augmentation recipe mixing competing speakers against foreground-only labels, no human annotation — and Mamba and LSTM backbones perform on par while longer-context attention does not help. Mamba-FVAD beats commercial VADs *and* enrollment-based speaker-aware systems on selectivity, with a new BG-FAR metric gated by foreground F1. Relevant to a six-hour take that is 2.0% speech with 43.5% of it in runs under 2 s.
- **[Voice-Light (arXiv:2609.20995, announced 21 Sep)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20995) — a full-duplex voice agent whose headline is a *withdrawn* capability claim, pre-registered.** On 1,673 real-conversation silence candidates a learned end-of-turn checkpoint held a 2.70% false-cutoff rate but reached only **12.53% recall against 95.60% for a plain Silero timing policy** — so they keep a hybrid controller and explicitly decline to claim a learned replacement. Median 758 ms from VAD endpoint to first audio over 36 turns, labelled an instrumented case study rather than an evaluation. Pairs with Saturday-the-12th's end-of-turn item (turn ends are prosodic, not semantic): **two independent results now say do not reach past Silero for turn boundaries.** Also worth the click for the invariant that cancelled audio must never enter durable history — the transcript-integrity analogue of delete-raw-audio-after-transcription.
- Two more, one line each. **[MECT (arXiv:2609.24061, 22 Sep)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24061)** adds Mixture-of-Experts to a CNN-Transformer speaker-verification backbone and, more usefully, reports a causal-retrained **streaming variant holding performance at a 100 ms chunk** — an alternative to ECAPA's wait-for-the-turn-to-end pooling. **[Word Timestamps and Speaker Attribution with a Non-Autoregressive LLM (arXiv:2609.15218, 15 Sep)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15218)** adds timestamps and speaker labels to an *already-produced* transcript, claiming SOTA timestamp accuracy and best cpWER **one to two orders of magnitude faster** than the autoregressive equivalent — i.e. attribution as a post-pass over faster-whisper output rather than a parallel pipeline.

## Memory & Extraction

- **[AIM + MUMBench (arXiv:2609.12320, 11 Sep)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12320) — privacy-scoped memory for a shared household agent, and its own numbers are the interesting half.** Classifies each memory as *private* (index-level access control, owner-only retrieval) or *public* rather than siloing per user; MUMBench is claimed as the first dataset evaluating retrieval/creation/update/deletion together in a multi-user setting. **96.0% visibility-classification accuracy but only 58.8% strict operation accuracy** — it decides who-may-see-what well and gets the actual memory operation right barely more than half the time. That split is the argument for your own shape: let the model classify, let code apply R1–R7.
- **[CueMem (arXiv:2609.12354, 11 Sep)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12354) — independent convergence on "spans are the ground truth, events are an index."** Treats extracted memory records as **retrieval cues, not evidence**: each cue links to its source turn, and at query time cues map back to turn anchors and expand over a turn graph to rebuild evidence from the original dialogue. Beats representative baselines on LoCoMo and LongMemEval while cutting query-time tokens and latency. **No numbers in the abstract** — gains stated qualitatively only, so this is architectural corroboration of `PHASE_7_MEMORY_DESIGN.md` §3, not a measured result to cite.
- **⚠️ NuMind's own published number contradicts your bake-off, and the direction matters.** The `numindai/nuextract` README still reports **`NuExtract3.4_4B-RL` at 0.651 ± 0.019** on their internal ~600-doc benchmark against **gemma-4-E4B-it at 0.538** — a purpose-built 4B extractor ~0.11 *above* your incumbent's base model on their data, which is the **opposite ordering** to your b10809 field addendum (E4B 0.7695 vs NuExtract3 0.6467 on yours). Both can be true — different corpora, different contract, and the addendum measured NuExtract3 as near-ceiling on person/household predicates and nearly blind to relations (`person.relation_to` F1 0.037), which is a corpus-composition effect. Worth recording as the reason a vendor benchmark is not a verdict. **The promised open-source NuExtract benchmark and leaderboard still has not shipped** (org page current as of 9 Sep); that is the thing to watch. [numindai/nuextract](https://huggingface.co/numind/NuExtract-2.0-8B)
- **[GraphProfiler (arXiv:2609.12448, EMNLP 2026 Main)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12448) — an existence proof that a provenance spine is nearly free, and a threat model for your inferred-facts layer.** Builds a source-linked personal knowledge graph where every node and edge traces to its originating post: **86.7% attribute-inference success on SynthPAI (within two points of text-only baselines) and 84.6% on PANDORA, while citing supporting evidence for >98% of predictions** — and ablations show removing the *cited* posts degrades success substantially more than removing an equal number of random ones, i.e. the citations are load-bearing rather than decorative. Read it twice: once as evidence that span-linked provenance costs almost nothing in accuracy, once as a description of what a household transcript corpus enables against its own residents.
