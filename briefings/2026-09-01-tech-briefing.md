**Lead — llama.cpp adopted semantic versioning and cut `v0.3.0` on Aug 25 — and that release *removes the `-no-cnv` CLI option* (#27542), the exact flag JARVIS's original 2026-04 bench-off used to score Gemma 4 E2B at 8.40/10.** Two things happened at once. (1) **Versioning:** `vX.Y.Z` tags are now the "stable" line with a slow cadence, while the familiar `b[NUM]` tags continue on ~every master commit as nightlies — so "which llama.cpp are you on" now has two valid answers, and GitHub's releases page marking a semver tag as *Latest* is part of why the b-tag-vs-`Latest` confusion these briefings logged through August looked so odd. (2) **The `-no-cnv` removal is the part that touches your repo.** `THINKING_MODE_RESEARCH.md` §7 records that the 8.40/10 was measured **raw-completion, explicit `-no-cnv`, no chat template** — a prompt shape the box has never used — and `MODEL_BENCH_2026-07.md` already showed that fixing the harness *changed the ranking* (E2B fell to third on its own workload; Llama 3.1 8B went from disqualified to first). **With the flag gone from the CLI, the original harness invocation is no longer reproducible on current llama.cpp** — so if a future re-bench wants raw-completion mode as a comparison arm, that now needs a pinned older build or a different mechanism, and it is worth pinning *before* the next model decision rather than discovering it mid-run. Also in `v0.3.0`: ggml bumped to v0.22.0, and **`mamba2: flatten in/out projections to dispatch GEMM instead of GEMV` (#27513)** — an SSM-path change in the same family as your gated DeltaNet work, though upstream's implementation, not yours. **Systems / OS**

- **The Zstd BMI2 finding is the best pure-performance item of the window, and it is your exact bug class: a CPU-feature probe sitting in a hot path.** In-kernel Zstd re-ran BMI2 detection *every time a compression/decompression context was created* — two serializing CPUID instructions with unconditional VM exits — and SquashFS calls that per block while EROFS/Btrfs/F2FS/crypto init a context per operation. Hoisting the probe to run once: **71% reduction in decompression time, 18% in compression** (crypto_acomp bench). Patches out for review Aug 26. Nothing to pull, but JARVIS does its own AVX2/XSAVE feature dispatch and this is the cleanest recent demonstration that *where* you put the check is worth more than the kernel you dispatch to. [Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/In-Kernel-Zstd-BMI2-Probing) · [patches](https://lore.kernel.org/lkml/20260826122558.2662013-1-usama.arif@linux.dev/)
- **NTFS3 local root via a crafted on-disk image, publicly disclosed Aug 22 and still unfixed in mainline at the time of writing.** Root cause is one line — `inode->i_mode = le32_to_cpu(value[2]);` in `fs/ntfs3/xattr.c` — loading `S_ISUID`/`S_ISGID` straight from untrusted on-disk data; udisks mounts NTFS `suid` by default, so plugging in a prepared USB gives any local user euid=0. The reason to read it: it is the same trust boundary as your FAT32 reader (SEC-028/029) and your GGUF loader's "owner-provisioned, therefore semi-trusted, therefore still bounds-check it" stance — a filesystem parser that believes a field because it is in the image. PoC public. [Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/NTFS3-Vulnerability-For-Root) · [disclosure thread](https://lore.kernel.org/ntfs3/CAGBKPgPiXyKWtjgYSACnugmG1XPs=mPg-Zu-xQziUZ1k921+qA@mail.gmail.com/T/)
- **Multikernel Linux had its first public release (7.0-mk2, Aug 25): several independent Linux kernels on one machine, bare metal, no hypervisor — each owning its own CPUs, memory and PCI devices, spawned via `kexec_file_load()`.** The author's framing is worth the read for its overlap with your own core-partitioning question: "no VM exit path, no second level of page tables and no device model… a lock, a panic or an exploit in one kernel cannot reach another." That is a monolithic-kernel project reinventing the isolation property seL4 gives you structurally — and it lands while the K/M4 carry-forward #3 (a dedicated PA core) is still open on your side, with the `JARVIS_PB_TICK` flip as its stated prerequisite. Mainline prospects unclear. [Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Linux-7.0-mk2-Multikernel) · [LKML announcement](https://lore.kernel.org/lkml/ao34RJ7aZ2BLd67S@pop-os.localdomain/)
- **Update (kernel-cycle watch, last touched Aug 13): Linux 7.2 shipped Aug 16 and 7.3-rc1 closed the merge window Aug 30 — the second-largest -rc1 in kernel history at 15,267 non-merge changesets, with the tree now at ~41 million lines.** The pending `apt upgrade` + reboot advice for the box's Ubuntu host (Zapscape KVM escape + Safe RET CVE-2026-68480 + SCTPhantom) can now ride a released 7.2 rather than a stable backport.
