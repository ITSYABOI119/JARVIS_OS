# JARVIS Daily Tech Briefing — Thursday, September 24, 2026

**Lead — [SpeakerMem-R1: Speaker-Centered Dual-Track Memory for Multi-Party Dialogue (arXiv:2609.26780)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26780) states, as its opening premise, the exact distinction your subject-gate rule already enforces — "who said what is different from what is true about whom" — and ships a benchmark for it.** Their complaint: conventional memory systems conflate the speaker (source) with the subject (owner), causing attribution errors in shared/multi-party settings. The fix is a **dual-track architecture**: a verbatim track (raw messages, speaker labels, timestamps, exact wording — an auditable ground truth) alongside a derived track of structured states across five layers (Episodic, Core, Profile, Interaction, Insight), each carrying explicit **owner/source fields and provenance links**. That verbatim-plus-derived split is your FTS5-lane-plus-extracted-facts shape, and the explicit owner-vs-source field is the generalized, externally-benchmarked version of your MS1a.3/.4 subject gate and the `about`-derivation change from contract 2. Results: **64.9% (SocialMemBench) / 47.9% (GroupMemBench) / 61.9% (EverMemBench) binary accuracy**, and their RL-trained local memory-writer model (Writer-R1) reaches **95.4% of a full LLM writer's performance** — a small local model doing the write-side job adequately, which bears directly on keeping the extractor local rather than reaching for a bigger model. Project page and benchmarks: [2022hpsk.github.io/SpeakerMemR1](https://2022hpsk.github.io/SpeakerMemR1/). **Denominator: memory-store-class architecture (runs beside the SQLite/FTS5/vector lanes, no model swap required) — nothing for the box.**

## Voice — verification, diarization, ASR

- **[Beyond Short Segments: Expanding Speaker Embeddings with Vector Archives (arXiv:2609.25007, INTERSPEECH 2026 oral)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25007) — lands squarely on the next planned step, speaker attribution of short utterances.** Kang, Cho and Kim extend speaker-embedding extraction beyond what a short segment alone supports by drawing on an archive of previously-extracted vectors rather than scoring the short clip in isolation. arXiv's rate limiting blocked a full read of the numbers this run (flagged, not skipped) — but the framing is a direct hit on the household pipeline's open problem: today's ECAPA-TDNN embeddings on short, often sub-2 s turns are exactly the "short segment" case this targets. Worth the full read before the short-utterance-attribution work starts. **Denominator: extractor-class (an archive of embeddings sits comfortably beside ECAPA on the 2070) — nothing for the box.**
- **[Speech Language Models for Full-Meeting Speaker Diarization: Capabilities and Limitations (arXiv:2609.23114, Sep 19, IEEE SLT 2026 — Li, Tian, Watanabe)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23114) — a negative-leaning result on the SpeechLM-does-diarization-end-to-end approach.** Comparing event-based (turn-timestamp) vs frame-based (per-frame speaker-activity) output representations from speech language models, they find event-based is more stable and consistent — but **full-meeting diarization remains bottlenecked by speaker tracking across the whole recording and by overlap**, and a separate speaker-linking post-process is needed to cut speaker confusion. Relevant as a "not yet" data point for anyone tempted to replace the current segment→embed→cluster pipeline with a single SpeechLM pass. **Denominator: extractor-class (GPU SpeechLM) — nothing for the box.**

## Memory & Extraction

- **[MemoryAthena: Adaptive Routing over Latent and Generated Memories (arXiv:2609.25853)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25853) — a measured argument for treating generated/inferred memory as conditionally useful rather than uniformly trustworthy.** Their framing: "generated memory... can complement direct retrieval in one context but interfere with it in another," so they train a small (~201M-parameter) causal routing head — on counterfactual future-token-likelihood advantages — to decide per-query whether to lean on direct retrieval, retrieval-conditioned generation, or backbone generation. Gains: QA five-task average 37.65 → 39.28, general NLP six-task average 76.73 → 79.13. Echoes the same instinct as your verbatim-span-over-synthesized-answer preference, this time with a learned router rather than a fixed rule. **Denominator: extractor-class (a small routing head beside the backbone, GPU) — nothing for the box.**
