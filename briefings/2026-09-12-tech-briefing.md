# JARVIS Daily Tech Briefing — Saturday, September 12, 2026

**Lead — whisper.cpp 1.9.4 shipped yesterday (Sep 11), and one line in its changelog is the defect you already found by hand: `whisper : re-seed decoder 0 between calls (#4025)`.** Decoder 0's sampling state was carrying across `whisper_full()` calls, so the same audio transcribed differently depending on what had been decoded before it — a cross-call determinism bug, invisible in any single run, invisible in WER. **That is precisely the M1b failure (`b37f272`): 3 segments once and 9 the other time on a byte-identical input, which is why the pipeline now pins `temperature 0.0 / beam_size 5 / vad_filter / condition_on_previous_text False` and runs a double-run guard that KEEPS audio whose two segmentations disagree.** The honest caveat is the one that matters: **you run faster-whisper on CTranslate2, not whisper.cpp — different codebase, so this is not your bug and there is nothing to pull.** What it is worth is external validation that "same bytes, different transcript" is an engine-level failure mode in the most-used local Whisper implementation, found and fixed by upstream — so the double-run guard is buying something real, not paranoia, and it is the right instrument to keep when CT2 is next upgraded. Same release also carries `whisper : default-initialize whisper_mel to avoid uninitialized read (#3981)` (another nondeterminism-shaped UB) and `parakeet : fix TDT decode by outputting raw logits from the joint graph (#4017)`. First whisper.cpp release in 22 days; syncs ggml 0.23.0. [1.9.4 changelog](https://freedom.tech/posts/2026-09-11-whisper-cpp-1-9-4/) · [release history](https://freedom.tech/project/whisper-cpp/)

## Voice — verification, diarization, ASR

- **Xiaomi-CocktailASR-1 (arXiv:2609.11274, Sep 10) is the owner-only-command architecture, published as one model.** LLM-based end-to-end target-speaker ASR that takes **reference speech as a voiceprint prompt** and transcribes only that speaker out of a mixture — no separation stage, no separate diarization — and, the part that matters most here, **it rejects: it outputs empty text when the target speaker is absent.** Your goal-8 rule is "commands OBEYED from the owner's verified voice ONLY, no wake word," which today is verify-then-transcribe as two stages with a duration gate between them; this collapses them and makes absence an explicit output rather than a threshold decision. They also claim single-speaker performance stays competitive, which is the usual cost of TS-ASR. **Denominator: extractor-class (GPU, LLM-scale ASR) — nothing for the box.** Weight availability is not stated in the abstract and I did not confirm it; treat as an architecture to read, not a model to download. [arXiv:2609.11274](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11274)
- **S4VAD + a probabilistic onset-latency framework (arXiv:2609.11110, Sep 10) — the first SSM-based VAD, and the evaluation half is the useful half.** Their argument: AUROC says nothing about *temporal responsiveness*, and onset-latency measurement is itself corrupted by noisy annotation timestamps — so they model annotated onsets as noisy observations of latent acoustic onsets and estimate the discrepancy distribution from data. S4VAD then controls the decay of past acoustic evidence directly, reaching the lowest latency of CNN/Transformer/RNN baselines at competitive AUROC. **Relevance: your turn boundaries come from Silero inside faster-whisper's `vad_filter`, and the 6-hour take measured 43.5 % of speech in runs < 2 s** — at that length an onset error is a large fraction of the embedding window, and nothing in your current instrumentation measures it. [arXiv:2609.11110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11110)
- **"Less can be More: What Aspects of Speech Drive End-of-Turn Detection" (arXiv:2609.11066, Sep 10, Stolcke et al.) — a controlled ablation finding that turn ends are prosodic, not semantic.** Acoustic+prosodic alone gives **utterance F1 0.93 at 7.8 % false alarms, 400 ms median latency**; **adding text increases premature detections without improving performance**, and feature-space analysis shows text representations overlap heavily across the boundary while prosodic features separate cleanly. **Why it lands: turns are your embedding unit** — MIN_CLIP_S 2 s to embed, ≥ 10 s of speech to ask the owner threshold — so how a turn is cut determines what gets scored. This says the cheap signal is the better one, i.e. don't reach for the transcript to fix turn segmentation. [arXiv:2609.11066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11066)
- **Diarization Error Decomposition Under Pause Annotation Ambiguity (arXiv:2609.11007, Sep 10, same group as yesterday's over-tightening paper).** Exact, overlap-aware split of DER into a **pause-attributable** component and a **core** component that proxies intrinsic error, leaving DER itself unchanged — and it shows the morphological closing everyone uses for pause-tolerant evaluation **discards segment-level distinctions**. Pairs directly with yesterday's item: that one named the hazard, this one gives you the measurement that separates "my boundaries disagree about silence" from "my model is wrong." On a household take that is 2.0 % speech, almost all of the error budget is the first kind. [arXiv:2609.11007](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11007)

## Memory & Extraction

- **ROAM (arXiv:2609.09778, Sep 9) is your write-rule discipline, measured — and its premise sentence is your design doc's.** The paper's complaint: existing systems "ask an LLM manager to add, update, delete, or rewrite memories directly, **coupling semantic interpretation, storage decisions, and content generation in one error-prone operation**." That is the Mem0-v3 scar (LLM UPDATE/DELETE dropped for ADD-only) stated as a design principle. ROAM instead has the LLM classify an incoming-vs-stored atom pair as **independent / equivalent / directionally subsuming / conflicting**, then code organises atoms into **Primary** (retrieved) and **Evidence** (supporting) roles, with fusion into compact non-atomic views. Results: **up to +29.8 points answer accuracy**, +15.6 points answer-critical source recall, −11.5 points confounder-token share, **robust across manager model scales**. **Take: it is the classify-then-apply-rules shape you already chose (`predicate_id` from a static registry, R1–R7 in code), with a number attached — and the Primary/Evidence split is a concrete answer to the crowding you measured at MS1a.2, where the planted preference sat at mean vector rank 78.5.** [arXiv:2609.09778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09778)
- **Bio-Memory: "Personalizing LLM Agent Memory Using Biometrics" (arXiv:2609.08558, Sep 8) — identity-conditioned memory retrieval in a shared-agent household, done with the wrong modality.** Built on A-Mem: every atomic memory note carries a **biometric embedding**, and biometric matching forms the retrieval **candidate pool before** semantic ranking. Evaluated on LoCoMo in a **10-user shared-agent** setting; owner-vs-non-owner separation reaches **27.29 % / 21.15 % F1 / BLEU-1** (face, CALFW) and **25.75 % / 19.22 %** (palmprint, MS_Blue). **The finding for you is the gap: they picked face and palmprint — the two modalities a household ambient system cannot use — and voice, the only one that works with a microphone in a room, is absent.** Your subject gate (MS1a.3/.4: a question naming a known person never takes another person's belief) is the exact-name version of this; conditioning the candidate pool on the speaker cluster is the embedding version, and nobody has published it. A companion paper the same day does it at the KV-cache layer ([BIO-MEMART, arXiv:2609.08566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08566)). [arXiv:2609.08558](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08558)
- **⚠️ Not new (April 2026) but never briefed, and it retro-explains MS1b's contract-2 result better than anything written since: "Schema Key Wording as an Instruction Channel in Structured Generation under Constrained Decoding" (arXiv:2604.14862).** Claim: under constrained decoding, **schema-key tokens enter the autoregressive context and act as instructions**, not just as structure — changing only key wording, with prompt, model, output structure and decoder fixed, substantially moves accuracy. And the measured split is startling next to your bake-off: **Qwen-family models benefit more from schema-level instruction, LLaMA-family models rely more on prompt-level guidance, and the two channels interact non-additively.** Your contract-2 change (first-person `about` derivation + the four-branch `oneOf`) moved **Gemma 4 E2B 0.3419 → 0.7201 and Llama 3.1 8B 0.4073 → 0.2944** — up for one family, *down* for the other, against a remap expectation of 0.4382. This is a published mechanism for exactly that asymmetry, and it says the field-verdict rule should be read as ranking *model × contract*, never model alone. [arXiv:2604.14862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14862)

## Privacy & Law

- **⚠️ Correction to yesterday's lead, from a law firm's read of the actual draft rather than the consultation paper: the exposure draft contains NO wearable-surveillance provisions.** Colin Biggers & Paisley, on the Tranche 2 draft: *"contrary to news articles, there are no proposed changes to directly deal with wearable surveillance (such as smart glasses), but rather they are measures still under development for which feedback is sought."* Yesterday's framing — regulator names your V1 wearable, on a clock — **overstated it**; the smart-glasses language lives in the consultation paper, not the bill. **What IS in the draft, and it is more relevant anyway:** biometric templates are added to **sensitive information** (consent-required, alongside genomic data and precise geolocation — so a speaker embedding is not merely "personal information", it is in the highest category); the personal-information definition widens to anything that lets someone be **"recognised or singled out"**, explicitly including behavioural patterns (your inferred facts); **APP 11 becomes security *and destruction*** — entities must consider destroying what they no longer need, identify that data, and regularly evaluate whether the practice works, i.e. **retention becomes an audited obligation, not a policy statement** (delete-raw-audio-after-transcription and the manual non-household purge are already that shape — what they lack is the audit trail the drafting implies); and consent gains a statutory five-part test (voluntary, informed, current, specific, unambiguous), which bundled or years-old consent will not satisfy. Small-business and employee-record exemptions survive. **Yesterday's open question — whether the s 16 personal/family/household-affairs exemption is touched — is STILL unresolved: this analysis's "what is not in the draft" section names only the small-business and employee-record exemptions and is silent on s 16.** Submissions close 18 September. [CBP analysis, 1 Sep](https://www.cbp.com.au/insights/publications/privacy-amendment-(personal-data-protection)-bill-2026-exposure-draft-released-for-second-tranche-o)
