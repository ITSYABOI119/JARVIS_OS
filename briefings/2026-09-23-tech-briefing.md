# JARVIS Daily Tech Briefing — Wednesday, September 23, 2026

**Lead — [Beyond the Stability–Plasticity Frontier in Streaming Target Speaker Extraction (arXiv:2609.20463, Sep 18)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20463) swaps a fixed-rule speaker-state update for a learned one, which is the same fix your single stored threshold (τ\* = 0.3461) has been missing across a dozen prior briefings.** The paper names the failure mode directly: heuristic embedding-update rules for streaming target-speaker extraction can't be both robust to target silence *and* adaptive to enrollment/mixture mismatch at the same time — a "stability-plasticity frontier." Their fix, **anchored fast-weights (AFW)**, is a 41k-parameter mechanism meta-trained end-to-end inside the closed streaming loop rather than hand-designed, so it sees realistic contaminated evidence instead of a clean assumption. Measured: **+3.0 dB over the best heuristic under severe enrollment/mixture mismatch, within 0.9 dB of static enrollment after 30 s of target absence, at under 5% runtime overhead** — and a GRU-based control confirms the gain is about the *learned-update* idea, not one specific architecture. This lands squarely on the next planned step — speaker attribution of short utterances — and it's the field's version of the argument these briefings keep making against a single fixed number: a small, per-speaker, per-moment adaptive state beats a static threshold, this time with a mechanism and a measured number rather than just a diagnosis. **Extractor-class hardware** (a 41k-parameter add-on runs beside ECAPA trivially on the 2070); nothing for the box.

## Voice — verification, diarization, ASR

- **[Samsone (arXiv:2609.21666, Sep 21)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21666) — a family of open small audio language models built explicitly for on-device inference: three sizes (99M / 134M / 356M), with weights, training code, mobile-optimized checkpoints, and an Android demo app all released together.** The 134M model is claimed state-of-the-art for its size class across multiple benchmarks — not independently verified here, and it's an audio-LM rather than a confirmed drop-in for faster-whisper. Worth tracking for the eventual continuous-recording wearable, where a sub-500M on-device model is exactly the right weight class; not actionable for the Main PC pipeline today.
- **[The Hidden Cost of Digits (arXiv:2609.21084, Sep 21)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21084) — number normalization alone moves WER by "more than 2 percentage points, and often higher than the differences between systems in popular multilingual benchmarks."** Practically relevant for a household assistant: times, addresses and quantities are exactly where a transcript silently degrades without the model changing. Worth confirming your own eval harness normalizes numerals consistently before trusting a WER delta between faster-whisper configs.
