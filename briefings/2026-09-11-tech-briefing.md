**Lead — Australia's exposure draft of the Privacy Amendment (Personal Data Protection) Bill 2026 is open for submissions and closes in seven days (18 September). Its accompanying consultation paper singles out, by name, "wearable devices that can discreetly record audio and video of bystanders who have no awareness they are being captured" — and separately proposes that collecting a *biometric template* requires consent.** That is goal 8's V1 wearable and goal 8's speaker-embedding store, described by the regulator, on a clock. Two clauses matter to the design rather than to compliance paperwork. (1) **The "fair and reasonable" test** replaces consent-as-a-free-pass: an organisation must show it could not achieve its purpose with less data, that a reasonable person would expect the collection, and that the subject had a genuine choice. Your §8 scope already concedes the hard version of this — everything transcribed, non-household transcripts purged *by hand* — and the test is the language in which that concession would have to be defended. (2) **A speaker embedding is a biometric template.** The owner enrolled himself, which is consent; the wife is *guessed*, and "everyone else listened to and learned from" is a cluster centroid built without one. **The honest caveat, stated because it probably decides the whole question: the Privacy Act's s 16 exemption for an individual acting "in connection with his or her personal, family or household affairs" is what would exempt a purely domestic JARVIS, and I could not confirm from the practitioner summaries whether tranche 2 touches it — flagged as unverified rather than assumed either way.** The summaries do agree the small-business exemption survives this tranche. Direction of travel, not an obligation today. ## Voice — verification, diarization, ASR

- **Over-Tightening-Aware Pseudo-Labeling for Tight-Boundary Speaker Diarization (arXiv:2609.09965, Sep 9, SLT 2026) — the most directly transferable voice paper of the window, and it names a hazard your ingest gate has.** Models trained on *loose* labels (padded boundaries, filled pauses) emit loose boundaries; the causal/anticausal pseudo-labelling fix for that **over-tightens**, and over-tightening raises missed detections which "propagate as unrecoverable errors to downstream tasks." Three remedies: remove pause *filling* rather than padding, a burn-in phase at the start of causal/anticausal predictions, and folding the non-causal model into co-training. **Why it lands on `phase7/voice`: your two-level owner rule keys on duration — the threshold is asked only of a turn holding ≥ 10 s of speech, and merge accrues ≥ 10 s of *embedded* speech.** A loose boundary inflates turn duration without adding speech, so padded silence can walk a turn through the 10 s gate and dilute the embedding it is scored on; a tight one drops speech you can never get back. Neither failure is visible in a WER number. [arXiv:2609.09965](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09965)
- **Pushing the Boundaries of Streaming Multi-Speaker ASR: A Systematic Study of Architectural Trade-offs (arXiv:2609.10265, Sep 10, NVIDIA, Interspeech 2026).** Categorises streaming multi-speaker ASR into **four** strategies by how diarization and ASR are integrated, builds one system per strategy (differing in whether they use multiple model instances, fine-tuning, or both) on Nemotron streaming RNN-T + **streaming Sortformer v2.1**, and scores each on multi-speaker accuracy, **single-speaker degradation, memory footprint and training complexity**. That last triple is the useful part: it is the decision table for "always listening, eventually 24/7 and live," which is exactly where your current offline pipeline (segment → transcribe → embed turns ≥ 2 s → cluster) has to go. Denominator note: Sortformer/Nemotron are NeMo, GPU, Apache-2.0 — **extractor-box class hardware (RTX 2070), not the seL4 box.** [arXiv:2609.10265](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10265)
- **A Unified Uncertainty-Aware Back-End for Speaker Verification (arXiv:2609.01221, Sep 1 — never briefed, and it is the M1a.4 problem with a method attached).** Premise: embeddings from real utterances have **trial-dependent reliability driven by duration, noise and channel** — which is precisely why your duration grid produced FRR 0.7413 → 0.0000 across D = 1…12 s at a fixed threshold, and why the 12.0 s minimum had to be retracted. Their answer is to carry a per-utterance covariance through the *whole* back-end: uncertainty-aware cosine scoring, **UAS-Norm** (cohort statistics adjusted by embedding uncertainty) and **UQMF** calibration. Your stack has none of this — a single stored τ* = 0.3461 from ECAPA point embeddings — and a per-trial reliability estimate is the principled alternative to a duration table you already retracted for small-n. No numbers quoted here: I could not reach the paper body (arXiv fetch rate-limited), so this is the mechanism, not a measured claim. [arXiv:2609.01221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01221)

## Memory & Extraction

- **Fortunate Recall: Ontology-Driven Memory Lifecycle Management (arXiv:2609.10413, Sep 11 — today, and it is your design with an external scoreboard).** Claim: memory systems treat every personal fact identically, so stores grow unbounded while precision degrades; lifecycle must be conditioned on **the behavioural type of the fact**. FR classifies facts into a **10+1 behavioural ontology** and applies per-category policies — differential temporal decay, slot-key supersession, event-time validity, category-aware retrieval routing — **as deterministic functions over LLM-extracted metadata.** That last clause is your own rule restated: the extractor *selects* `predicate_id` from a static registry and the code applies R1–R7. Results: **76.9 % on LifecycleBench** (a new 516-question temporal-disambiguation benchmark) against Mem0 / A-MEM / Memory-R1 / MemoryOS at **61–70.5 %**, and 75.2 % on LongMemEval-S — with **no measurable retrieval cost** from the lifecycle layer. **Two things to take: the result is direct evidence that typed-per-predicate beats uniform retention, and LifecycleBench is a ready-made external set to run beside your pre-registered household bands** (which are synthetic and self-authored — an outside benchmark is the thing they cannot supply). [arXiv:2609.10413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10413)
- **Bitemporal validity windows, measured: Zep/Graphiti scores 63.8 % vs Mem0's 49.0 % on LongMemEval temporal reasoning — a 15-point gap the comparison attributes to storing *fact validity windows* rather than timestamps.** That is `valid_from` + non-destructive supersession versus "newest write wins," and it is the single largest design choice in `PHASE_7_MEMORY_DESIGN.md` §3. **Sourcing caveat, and it cuts in an unusual direction: this is published by Mem0 — a vendor reporting its own loss, which is more credible than the reverse — but the page carries no date I could pin, so treat the direction as evidence and the digits as unverified.** [Mem0 benchmark write-up](https://mem0.ai/blog/ai-memory-benchmarks-in-2026)
- **Nothing net-new on constrained decoding** in the Sep 9–11 window. Standing state, recorded once so it need not be re-derived: XGrammar is the default structured-output backend for vLLM/SGLang/TensorRT-LLM at **< 40 µs/token**, llguidance's Rust Earley parser ~50 µs/token, and on JSONSchemaBench the invalid-JSON rate is **2.21 % (XGrammar) vs 0.12 % (llguidance)** once degeneration is excluded — while Outlines' FSM compilation (40 s to 10+ min on complex schemas) makes it the lowest-compliance engine by timeout. **Relevance to MS1b's contract-2 result: your eleven-model bake-off reached validity 1.0000 on nine of eleven runs through llama.cpp's own grammar path, so the engine is not your bottleneck — the four-branch `oneOf` was.** [llguidance](https://github.com/guidance-ai/llguidance) · [JSONSchemaBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10868v1)

## Wearable Hardware

- **Not news — first mention of this beat, logged so V1 does not start from a blank page. [ESP32-OpenMic](https://codeberg.org/RevK/ESP32-OpenMic) is the closest existing open reference design to your stated V1:** open-source firmware *and* PCBs for I2S mic + speaker, explicitly including "a self-contained battery-operated audio recorder to micro SD card," tested against the **TDK ICS-43434 (24-bit I2S MEMS)**. Nearest ESP32-**S3** sibling is [ESPERDYNE](https://zenodo.org/records/18021127) — a 192 kHz dual-channel recorder with a PSRAM ring buffer and single-tap WAV capture to microSD (Dec 2025, bat-call listener, but the capture path is the same shape). Radios-off, continuous-record, USB-C-ingest is a firmware decision on either base, not a new board.
