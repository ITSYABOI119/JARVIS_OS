# JARVIS Daily Tech Briefing — Wednesday, September 9, 2026

**Lead — the mystery x86 vendor disclosed on Sep 7 that its shipping silicon is *PM64-only, PML5-only* — no Legacy Mode, no Compatibility Mode — with FRED, APX and x86S, "used at scale for >1 year."** Christian Ludloff relayed the message to LKML; it is the fourth such disclosure from the same unnamed corporate entity (after the opcode/CPUID/MSR allocations, the 16/32-tile AMX implementations that already exceed shipping Xeon AMX, and the Aug-29 `WBINVD` prefix-hint advisory this briefing led with on Sep 2). The new content is the architectural stance: **Intel abandoned x86S in mid-2024 and someone else picked it up and shipped it** — "it is time to drop the ballast," "migrate your complexity to x86 software emulation." **Why this is a bare-metal-OS item and not a CPU-gossip item: a PM64-only part has no 16-bit real mode and no 32-bit protected mode, which is the entire early-boot path JARVIS inherits.** The box boots GRUB → multiboot → seL4's `boot_sys.c`, and that chain exists because x86 starts in real mode; on such a part it does not start there at all. Nothing to do — the 2700X is Zen+ and this is one anonymous vendor — but it is the first concrete sign that "x86 bare metal" may stop meaning "inherits four decades of mode transitions," and that is a load-bearing assumption in every bootloader this project depends on. Pairs with the Sep-4 ACE/GCC-17 marker: the ISA target for a *future* box is moving on two axes at once, and neither is AVX2.
