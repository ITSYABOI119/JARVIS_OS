# JARVIS Daily Tech Briefing — Monday, September 7, 2026

**Lead — llama.cpp cut `v0.4.0` on Sep 4, and the headline feature is a model-load change that lands squarely on your loader: `--lazy-mode` / on-demand tensor reading (#27794), plus a separate "prevent RAM peaking at model loading stage" (#27483).** Never briefed — Friday's run reported the surrounding nightlies (b10791, b10794) and the semver release itself slipped past the sweep. The core of it is `llama_lazy_mode`: tensors are read from the GGUF *when needed* rather than pulled in wholesale at load, with a companion fix for the transient RAM spike during the load itself. **Why it matters here more than to most llama.cpp users: JARVIS's load path is the one you hand-wrote, and its cost is measured, not hypothetical.** PA reads the whole `GEMMA2B.GUF` off FAT32 into a mapped region before PB ever serves a query; the boot-smoke generation leg measured 807 MB through the emulated NVMe at **117 / 161 / 139 s** across three runs, and provisioning `QWENEMB.GUF` added **+609 MB on top of Gemma's 2962 MB = +20.6 %** of load I/O for a capability that is idle most of the time. Upstream is now shipping the obvious lever against exactly that: don't read what you have not been asked for. **The honest caveat is that it is a direction marker, not a patch to lift** — your frames are allocated and mapped up front by design (the `[VMAP]` non-overlap proof, the `embed_base_for` runtime derivation, `model_frame_caps[]` preserved across a K/M2 respawn all assume a resident model), and "warm model survives the respawn" is the property K/M2a locked. A lazy loader trades that for boot time. Worth reading before the embedder's load cost next gets questioned. Also in 0.4.0: ggml → **v0.23.0** (sparse flash attention, RPC async APIs), a **quantizer RAM cap** (`max_buf_size`, #27795) and **row-slab streaming** (#27830), and `--n-cpu-ffn` (#26622). **Quantization**

- **Never briefed (submitted Sep 1): "The Structure of Quantization Damage in LLMs: Why the Next Bit Should Be Spent Globally" (arXiv 2609.01587) — and the result is a direct argument against the whole layer-selective mixed-precision family.** Method is causal, not correlational: raise each layer to 8-bit *one at a time* and measure the accuracy it actually recovers, across 9 open-weight models in 4 architecture families. **Three intuitive hypotheses about where quantization damage lives — task circuits, where the model computes, weight statistics — none of them predicts which layers benefit.** Recovery is diffuse: for **8 of 9 models, recovering 75 % of the accuracy gap takes roughly half the layers** (Qwen3-8B is the lone concentrated exception). And at a matched precision budget, **spending it globally on finer quantization granularity beats locally repairing the most recoverable layers by 21–52 points, on all 8 group-128-compatible models — including the concentrated one.** Secondary finding worth its own line: **8-bit is near-lossless in their evaluation across RTN, GPTQ and AWQ**, so the residual is budget-limited rather than method-limited. **Why it lands on your desk:** the box deploys Q4_K_M, whose K-quants *are* the finer-granularity story (per-block scales, group-128-shaped), and this is a measured argument that the deployed default is the right shape and that a "protect the important layers" scheme is the wrong place to spend a bit. Pairs with the SemKV 2.322-bit cliff on the KV side: same lesson, opposite axis — bits bought globally, cliffs respected. Read, not a lever. [arXiv 2609.01587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01587) · [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01587v1)

- **Sep 6: a Coccinelle-driven tree-wide sweep landed in 7.3-rc2 converting hundreds of files / 600+ lines of `kmalloc()` to `kmalloc_obj()` — and the rationale is a C-idiom argument, not a kernel-specific one.** `ptr = kmalloc(sizeof(*ptr), gfp)` becomes `ptr = kmalloc_obj(*ptr, gfp)`: no `sizeof`, no type name, and the helper returns a pointer *to the type of its first argument* rather than `void *`, so a mismatched assignment fails to compile. Kees Cook's stated forward motive is the part to note — type introspection at the allocation site enables **alignment-aware allocator choices and type-sensitive hardening** later. Your codebase is full of the old idiom and has already paid for size/type drift once (the `qmodel_forward` ~8 KB stack budget and the `fwd_scratch` rule exist because a large temporary's size was implicit). No action; a pattern worth stealing if a JARVIS allocation wrapper ever gets written. [Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Linux-7.3-rc2-kmalloc-obj) · [the introducing commit](https://git.kernel.org/pub/scm/linux/kernel/git/torvalds/linux.git/commit/?id=2932ba8d9c99875b98c951d9d3fd6d651d35df3a)
